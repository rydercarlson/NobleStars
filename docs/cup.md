# Nobles Cup

The 3v3 Brawl Ball mode. Split out of `CLAUDE.md`; read this before touching `cup_mode.gd`, `ball.gd` or `Arena.PITCH_MAP`.

**Nobles Cup** (`cup_mode.gd`, `ball.gd`, `Arena.PITCH_MAP`) — the Brawl Ball mode, 3v3, first to two goals or ahead when the 2:30 clock runs out. Menu id `nobles_cup` → engine mode `"cup"`; `NS3_MODE=cup` alongside `NS3_KIT` drops straight into one.

- **Everything mode-specific is in `cup_mode.gd`**, which main.gd calls at four points: `build_match`, `tick`, `on_death` and `frozen` (the last holds input through a kickoff and after the whistle). Showdown's match loop is otherwise untouched, and `cup == null` is the guard on every branch.
- **Teams are real.** `Fighter.team` is 0/1 (−1 = Showdown free-for-all, where `is_ally` is false for everyone). `deal_damage` and `nearest_visible_enemy` both check it, so friendly fire and friendly targeting are off everywhere at once. Team is worn as a coloured ground ring — it can't tint the body, because eight of nine kits wear a GLB whose materials are the character's. **That ring is now drawn in every mode**, sized to the collision radius, which is how Brawl Stars shows a hitbox ("the ring around their feet, not the Brawler themselves"); in Showdown, where there is no team, it takes the kit colour. Health bars go green for your side and red for theirs (`fighter_bars.gd`); Showdown keeps kit colours.
- **Death is a setback, not an exit.** `_eliminate` hands off to `CupMode.on_death`, which parks the fighter (`Fighter.knock_out`) and books a respawn three seconds later; `fighters` never shrinks, and everything that walks it already skipped `is_dead()`. Overtime is sudden death and books nothing.
- **You respawn inside your own goal**, not at the kickoff spot — a body in the mouth is where the mode's defending comes from. There is deliberately no goalkeeper bot; an early build had one and it was strictly worse than the wall. Kickoff spawns are on the middle third, two tiles apart, so the whole team is on screen at the start.
- **The pitch does the defending.** 21x33 tiles — Brawl Stars' own 3v3 size, now that a tile is a body-width — mirrored on both axes (every row is a palindrome — keep it that way). A solid seven-tile breakable wall sits three rows off each goal, covering the five-tile mouth completely, so nothing can be shot at the mouth from beyond it and the attack has to come round the outside. **The two spawn rows must straddle `row_count / 2`** (integer division, 16 on 33 rows): `arena.gd` assigns team by that test alone, so a pitch whose spawn rows both land one side of it puts both teams in one half. Overtime levels every breakable wall, that one included, which is what opens the goals up. Only the `=` goal frame survives. Both teams spawn on the middle third, so a kickoff is a race.
- **The ball is not a physics body.** It reflects off `Arena.blocks_movement` per axis, so bounces are exact and reproducible; a RigidBody3D would creep into wall seams and squeeze the posts. It is caught by anyone it rolls within `PICKUP_RADIUS` of, which is what makes interception work.
- **The carry is clamped off walls** (`Ball._carry_point`). A fighter's capsule is 0.65 m and the ball is held 0.85 m in front, so a carrier pressed against a wall held it *inside* that tile — and at chest height the top of the ball (1.67 m) clears `Arena.WALL_HEIGHT`, so it surfaced through the top and read as floating over the wall. The carry is walked in until the ball is clear; `kick()` launches from that same point, which is also what stopped a point-blank kick starting the ball inside a wall, where `_advance` reverses it on both axes every frame and it never gets out.
- **The carrier can't attack at all, only kick.** A normal kick spends an ammo bar and obeys the same attack cooldown as a shot, as it does in Brawl Ball, so the ball cannot be machine-gunned up the pitch and running dry is a real reason to hold on to it. It runs 7.8 m — **6 tiles, and a tile is a fighter, so six body-widths** — deliberately inside the 8.5-tile weapon range cap, so kicking the ball never outreaches shooting it: a kick is a pass, not a shot from anywhere. **This sentence described ~7 m for months while the code ran 15.3 m**, and nothing caught it because the Cup camera only ever showed two thirds of the pitch width; locking that camera on 7 Sep 2026 made the whole pitch visible and it was reported from play within one match as "way too far/fast". Retune the DISTANCE by moving `Ball.DRAG`'s multiplier against `KICK_SPEED`'s — both derive from `Kits.SPEED_NORMAL`, which cancels out of `kick_range()`, so the speed dial changes how long a kick takes and never how far it goes. Spending the Super on the ball is the **Super Shot**: `Ball.SUPER_KICK_MULT` doubles the launch speed, and because drag is constant that doubles the distance too (~16 m, 12.4 tiles), matching Brawl Ball's "shoots it further and faster, but uses up the Super". It costs the Super and no ammo, so it is also the carrier's out when the magazine is empty. While carrying, the Super never fires the kit's Super, and an uncharged one is swallowed rather than falling through to an attack. Every player attack funnels through `main.gd:_fire_player`, which is the backstop; `_release_fire`/`_auto_aim_fire` resolve the aim first (drag aims, tap uses `CupMode.kick_aim`, which shoots inside `SHOT_RANGE` — doubled for a Super Shot — and otherwise passes). The aim indicator switches to the ball's own path, bounces and all, via `_aim_bounce_segments(..., decay)`; the Super Shot's lane is gold and twice as long.
- **The ball is dropped by a dash, a leap, or a knock that came from a SUPER**, on top of death. Brawl Ball's own rule is stun/knockback/defeat; dashes and jump-smashes are added because otherwise every mobility Super is a free run at the goal. **"From a Super" is a comparison, not a threshold, and that is load-bearing**: this used to be a bare speed test on `knockback_vel`, and Kovacs' clap shoves at 4.0 — over `KNOCK_DROP_SPEED` — so a *basic attack* on a normal reload stripped the carrier every time it landed. No constant can fix that, because Sanjit's **Super** also shoves at exactly 4.0. So `deal_damage` records the attacker on the target (`Fighter.knock_from`/`knock_strength`/`knock_dir`) and `CupMode._knock_was_super` asks whether the impulse beat that attacker's OWN `weapon.knockback`. Every kit gives its Super more knockback than its attack, so this stays correct for a kit added later with nothing retuned. The ball keeps a capped share of the shove (`Ball.knock_loose`, `KNOCK_BALL_MAX` ≈ 6 m against a kick's 15) so a strip reads as knocked away without ever becoming a shot; `last_touch` is deliberately unchanged, since whoever landed the Super never touched the ball. `Fighter.forget_knock()` spends the shove the instant it takes the ball — a 10 m/s knock decays over ~0.58s against a 0.35s pickup hold, so without it the carrier re-collected while still wearing a live knock record and was stripped three times off one Super.
- **A Cup tap tells you which of its two jobs it is about to do.** `CupMode.kick_plan` returns `{kind, at, dir}` with kind `shot` / `pass` / `clear`, and `main.gd` draws a ring on `at` — the goal, or the team-mate. The rule was never wrong; the tell was missing, so a tap silently did one of two very different things depending on a distance the player could not see. `kick_aim` remains the direction-only half for callers that just kick, and the bots read the same plan, so the ring and the tap cannot drift apart.
- **Bots route around the goal wall** rather than into it. `Arena.route_to_goal` is a BFS distance field per goal, rebuilt only when a wall opens, with a five-tile lookahead so the run is a curve and not a corner-hug. Steering straight at the mouth parked carriers against the wall until someone took the ball off them; measured after the fix, carriers deviate >40° from the straight line exactly at 6-9 m out, which is where the wall is.
- **A destroyed wall leaves the ASCII map** via `Arena.open_at`, called from the projectile hit, the net RPC and overtime. Bot movement, route finding and the ball's bounces all read `Arena.rows`, so a wall only freed from the scene keeps blocking every one of them — overtime would otherwise leave the ball rebounding off gaps you can see through. This applies to Showdown too, where bots previously kept pathing around walls that were no longer there.
- **Pacing lives in four constants.** `Ball.KICK_SPEED`/`DRAG`, `Ball.SUPER_KICK_MULT`, `CupMode.SHOT_RANGE`, and the goal wall. Retune those before touching anything else, and re-measure with a batch of headless matches rather than one — variance between single matches is large. All three ball constants **derive from `Kits.SPEED_NORMAL`**, which cancels out of `kick_range()`: the game's speed dial changes how long a kick takes and never how far it goes. Retune the DISTANCE with `DRAG` against `KICK_SPEED`, and the PACE with `STOP_SPEED`.
- **THE TEST FOR THE BALL IS WHETHER IT BEATS A RUNNER**, not how fast it leaves the boot — `dist / time` against `Kits.SPEED_NORMAL`. For the whole life of the project it did not: every tuning ever shipped averaged **0.62-0.76x a fighter's run** over the length of a kick, because exponential decay spends most of its time in the tail, so the ball launched fast and then trickled and a defender could jog after any pass. It was reported as "kick still too slow" only after the launch speed had been cut twice for it — **the launch was never the problem.** `Ball.STOP_SPEED` was: ending the roll at 0.42x a run instead of 0.11x cuts the tail, and the same 8-tile kick lands in 2.0 s instead of 4.6, averaging 1.45x a run. The old "a fast ball made every shot a goal and ran a match out in thirteen seconds" is still true of the LAUNCH speed and is why it is 3.5x a run rather than more.
- **A tapped kick locks onto the GOAL and nothing else.** It used to hunt for the team-mate nearest the goal, which is a lock-on: it picks a receiver for you and the ball leaves in a direction you cannot read off your own facing — the same objection recorded against the attack indicator previewing auto-aim's pick. In range of the goal it aims at the goal; otherwise it goes where you are facing. `CupMode.kick_plan`'s `allow_pass` is false for the player and **true for bots**, which need it: a bot has no drag to fall back on and a 3v3 where nobody passes is not the mode. The indicator marks where the ball STOPS ROLLING, drawn always, and that ring is deliberately smaller than the goal ring — one is a distance read-out, the other is a target, and they must not read alike.
- **The Cup camera is pulled back AND LOCKED HORIZONTALLY** (`Arena.CUP_CAM_PULLBACK`, `Arena.cam_anchor`). Brawl Ball never scrolls sideways; both touchlines are always visible. **Pulling back alone is not enough** — a wider window that still follows the player in X loses a touchline just the same, only more slowly. Two traps: the camera's position and its `look_at` target must come from the SAME anchor, or it yaws to face an off-centre player and — since rotation is fixed at match start — renders the whole pitch visibly TILTED for the entire match; and the framing hid a real balance bug for months, because a camera showing two thirds of the pitch is a camera you cannot check a kick's range against.
- **The goal is a net on the floor plus a real frame.** `Arena._build_goal` derives everything from `goal_mouths` — never from PITCH_MAP row numbers — so a wider mouth or a second pitch gets its goal for free. The mouth floor is a team-tinted grid that darkens toward the back, and that gradient is the whole depth cue: the recess is one tile deep and the camera is nearly overhead, so there is no other way to see into it. Posts and crossbar stand on the goal line at `GOAL_FRAME_HEIGHT`, deliberately taller than `WALL_HEIGHT` — a bar level with the wall behind it disappears into it at this pitch.
- **Wifi play: the host runs the rules, a client's CupMode is a presentation layer.** `CupMode.authoritative` mirrors main.gd's, and `tick()` branches on it into `_client_tick` before any rule runs — no pickups, no goal test, no carrier check, no clock, no respawns. Same split `GasRing` already uses, where a client builds the node and never calls `start()`.
  - **State rides the snapshot; EVENTS ride reliable RPCs.** The score, clock, freeze, overtime/finished flags, the carrier's roster index and a loose ball's position are state. A goal, a knock-out, a respawn, a kickoff and a kick are events — each happens once and is unrecoverable from a later packet, exactly like Showdown's elimination feed. `CupMode.goal_effects` is the presentation half of `_goal_check`, split out so the host and a client run the *same* code and cannot drift.
  - **The carrier rides as an INDEX, not as the ball's position.** A client then draws the ball through its own `Ball._carry_point`, which is what makes the ball track its own *predicted* fighter with no round trip when the client is carrying. A LOOSE ball is interpolated in `_net_render_puppets` like a puppet, not applied in `_apply_snapshot_state` — a Super Shot leaves at 28 m/s, nearly a metre between snapshots, so taking it off the newest packet shows as a ball stepping across the grass.
  - **A client's own PICKUP is applied off the newest packet, not the delayed one.** Everything else about the ball can afford `NET_INTERP_DELAY`, because everything else about it is something you are watching; running onto a loose ball is the thing you are *doing*, and 85 ms on top of the round trip before it sticks to your hands is most of what made the mode feel remote. `CupMode.apply_local_carry` owns the local case entirely and `apply_net_state` returns early for it — otherwise an older packet walks the pickup straight back and the ball sticks, then comes loose a frame later. Same rule the client's own health and ammo already follow.
  - **The host must not drive a remote player through a kickoff freeze.** `_run_playing`'s `if net_host` block was the only one of the three movement blocks not gated on `held`: the freeze zeroes everyone's movement at the top of the function, and this then put the remote player's LAST input back on top, because `_consume_input` returns the previous input when the queue is empty and a frozen client has stopped sending. Their fighter crept across the pitch on the host while standing still on their own screen, and the freeze lifting brought it back as a correction. Measured 2-3 cm average prediction error with 29 cm spikes in Cup against a flat 0 in Showdown, which has no freeze for it to happen in; flat 0 in both after the gate.
  - **A client kicks by ASKING.** `_fire_player`'s "am I a client" test now comes *before* the `cup.kick` backstop — with it after, a client moved the ball on its own screen and never told anybody. The host runs the identical `cup.kick` inside `_net_fire`, and echoes `_net_cup_kick` only when the ball actually left the carrier's hands (which it tests by `ball.carrier != f`, since `kick()` also returns true when it swallowed the input for want of ammo). Aiming stays local: `kick_plan` is pure geometry over data the client already has.
  - **Anything that teleports the client's own fighter must call `_net_resync_prediction()`.** `_net_predict` starts each frame with `player.position = _pred_pos`, so a kickoff or a respawn that moved the body without moving the prediction is undone on the first unfrozen frame — the fighter snaps back to where it died, holds for a round trip, then gets dragged forward. It reads as the respawn not working. The client also skips prediction entirely while `cup.frozen(now)`.
  - **`CupMode._kick_spot` is per-fighter, replacing the two `_lineup` arrays.** build_match and kickoff used to walk those with separate counters, and when the counters disagreed two fighters landed on one tile and the depenetration fired them hundreds of metres up. Storing the answer per fighter instead of the recipe for computing it twice makes that impossible, and it is what lets a client agree with the host — the host simply sends each fighter's spawn index in the roster.
  - **The results board is DATA, not six live fighters.** `_cup_rows()` builds `{name, kit, team, goals, kills, damage, idx, you}` and the host broadcasts it in `_net_cup_over`; a client's own `Fighter.stats` are all zeros. Two traps it already hit: the name must come off the **roster**, never `display_name`, because every machine calls its own fighter "You" and a broadcast table otherwise gives everyone a team-mate literally called YOU; and the "YOUR TEAM" column is `player.team`, not team 0, because over wifi you may well be on team 1.
  - **A player who leaves is handed to a bot brain** rather than eliminated. Showdown can afford to remove them; a 3v3 cannot, and their side would play the rest of the match two-against-three.
  - Room capacity is `Net.room_capacity()` — six in Cup, ten in Showdown — and the host picks the mode in `room_screen.gd` before pressing HOST. Humans fill one side before the other, the way a party does in Brawl Stars.
