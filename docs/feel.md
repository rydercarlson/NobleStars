# Match feel

Haptics, effects, touch controls, aiming, match bookends and the lineup. Split out of `CLAUDE.md`.

## Haptics

**Haptics** (`haptics.gd`, static class `Haptics`) — the phone's half of the feedback layer, and the third thing after the SFX and the VFX that ships no asset file.

- **Godot's iOS backend emits ONE event type and ONE parameter, and everything here follows from that.** `Input.vibrate_handheld` reaches `AppleEmbedded::vibrate_haptic_engine` (`drivers/apple_embedded/apple_embedded.mm`), which builds a Core Haptics pattern holding a single `CHHapticEventTypeHapticContinuous` with only `HapticIntensity` set — never `CHHapticEventTypeHapticTransient`, never `HapticSharpness`. So the engine can express duration and strength and nothing else. A table of duration/amplitude pairs is therefore N lengths of ONE sensation, which is why the first pass read as undifferentiated: a menu tap and a goal had the same texture because there is only one texture. Getting a real click or a timbre axis needs a native plugin, which is written up in todo.md.
- **The missing axis is bought back as RHYTHM.** Core Haptics mixes concurrent players and GDScript can schedule calls, so a tap is a SCORE — `[[offset ms, duration ms, amplitude], ...]` — with the head fired on the frame and the tail queued. A hard 20 ms segment followed by a soft 60 ms one reads as an impact where one 80 ms block reads as a hum; a goal is three rising thumps. Every shape in `SCORES` is built out of that one idea. The queue drains in `_process`, so segments carry a frame of jitter (measured over a Cup match: 0-8 ms, median 4; a 60 fps phone tops out around 16) — which is why **nothing places two segments closer than ~40 ms**.
- **Three device classes, probed, not assumed.** `OS.has_feature("mobile")` is also true on an iPad, which has no haptic engine at all, so `_device()` asks the platform singleton — `AppleEmbedded` (or `iOS` on older templates) binds `supports_haptic_engine()` into ClassDB. TAPTIC gets the scores; BUZZ collapses every score to its loudest segment and drops any that peaks under `BUZZ_MIN_AMP`, because `AudioServicesPlaySystemSound` ignores both arguments and answers everything with the same ~0.4s buzz — a `ui_tap` would arrive as a 0.4s buzz on every menu press. **That fallback is a real constraint that this build never meets**, since it side-loads onto a modern iPhone, and designing the whole vocabulary around it was the first pass's other mistake.
- **Tiers, not just spacing.** The old throttle was first-come-first-served inside `MIN_GAP`, so a menu tap could swallow a death — the killing blow fires `hit` from the health watch and `death` from `_eliminate` in the same frame, and which survived depended on frame ordering. Every entry now carries a `Tier` (PICKUP / EVENT / CEREMONY / MATCH) and a higher tier preempts a lower one, clearing whatever it had queued. **The axis is what happened in the match, not what it cost you**: firing your Super is an EVENT and the elimination it produced is a CEREMONY, which is load-bearing — ranked the other way the kill was measurably eaten, since a Super that kills lands both inside 160 ms.
- **`Haptics.warm()` at match start and on entering the menu.** Godot builds the engine with `setAutoShutdownEnabled:true`, so it idles down between taps and the first one after a lull pays the start-up cost — and a Showdown lull is easily ten seconds, which makes the late tap exactly the one that mattered. The queue needs a frame signal, so a single hidden `Pump` node is parked on the scene ROOT (surviving the match/menu swap without having to become an autoload and rename every call site); it also stops the engine on `NOTIFICATION_APPLICATION_PAUSED`.
- **The damage curve is measured and was re-measured.** `HIT_SOFT`/`HIT_HARD` (0.06–0.34) replace the old single `HEAVY_FRACTION` threshold: damage dealt runs 8-28% of the target's max with a median of 14%, a gas tick is a flat 17%, and a shot lands for 9-29% by kit and range — so essentially everything lives between 6% and a third. A first pass at 0.05–0.45 spent over half the amplitude range on damage that never occurs and put every real hit between 0.36 and 0.61 of a scale running to 0.98. Re-measure with `NS3_HAPTIC_LOG` before touching these.
- **Damage taken is watched in `main.gd:_update_status`, not hooked into `deal_damage`.** A wifi client never runs `deal_damage` at all (`authoritative` returns it early) and takes its health off the snapshot stream, so reading the number frame to frame is the one hook that covers both — and a nine-pellet shotgun arrives as one exchange.
- **Damage DEALT is banked over `LANDED_WINDOW`, not a frame.** A shotgun's pellets are nine projectiles with their own flight times, so they land across three or four frames: banking per frame still collapsed to one tap (the repeat gap), but that tap carried the first frame's share and read as a third of the blow it was. `landed` is deliberately a different RHYTHM from `hit` — two even ticks against a thump-and-tail — and capped well below it, because what happens TO you must always outweigh what you do to someone else.
- **The aim stick has a detent** (`main.gd:_update_aim_detent`): `aim_on`/`aim_off` fire as `value` crosses `TAP_THRESHOLD`, which is where the stick stops meaning "tap to auto-aim" and starts meaning "this is the lane". The indicator does change with it, but the indicator is under your thumb while your eyes are on the fight. Two details are load-bearing. It is watched **per frame, not off `_unhandled_input`** — a drag event only arrives while the finger is MOVING, so a thumb that crosses the line and holds still stops generating them. And the anti-chatter is a **dwell, not hysteresis**: a hysteresis band would make the tap claim a state the game is not in for the width of the band, whereas a dwell only declines to report a crossing that did not last. A release resets the state silently, since the shot that follows has its own feedback.
- **Only ever your own events.** Every hook is gated on the local player — a bot dying across the map is a sound, not something your hand should know about.
- **`REPEAT_GAP` sits on top of `MIN_GAP` for taps that arrive as a stream.** A gas burn ticks about twice a second, a sustained magazine lands two to five connects a second, and held fire on a dry magazine clicks 2.4 times a second — that last one is fine to hear and a rattle to feel, so `empty` is gated at 1.2 s (four times the sound's own gate) and pairs with `ammo_ready`, which fires only on the pip that takes you from empty back to able to shoot rather than on every pip.
- **Three instruments, because none of this can be felt on a desktop.** `NS3_HAPTIC_LOG=1` prints every tap with its score, tier and gap — and prints the DROPS with their reason, which is how you tell a throttle from a missing hook. `NS3_HAPTIC_AUDIO=1` renders each score as a low tone burst through a plain `AudioStreamPlayer`: rhythm is the thing being designed and it survives the translation to sound, so shapes can be compared without a device install each time. `NS3_HAPTIC_DEMO=<name>` loops one entry, for tuning on the phone itself.
- Toggled by `SaveGame.haptics_on` (Settings, beside Music and SFX). The row shows on desktop as well, where it can do nothing: the save travels to the phone with the build and that is the only place it can be set.
- **The menu's entire haptics is one line in `MenuUI.press_feedback`**, which every pressable already goes through, plus `MenuShell.HAPTIC_SOUNDS` for the handful of grant sounds a press does not cover.

## Match bookends

**Match bookends** (`loading_screen.gd` autoload `Loading`, `versus_screen.gd`, and the results half of `main.gd`) — what wraps a match at both ends.

- **Entering and leaving a match go through `Loading`,** never `change_scene_to_file` directly. It is an autoload precisely so it outlives the swap it is covering: a loading screen owned by the scene being replaced dies halfway through the job. `Loading.to_match(get_tree())` (from `menu.gd:start_match`) and `Loading.to_menu(get_tree())` (LOBBY, and a lost host) threaded-load the target scene **plus every character GLB**, keep a reference to each in `_held` for the life of the session — which is what makes `fighter.gd`'s later `load(kit.model)` a cache hit instead of a disk read on the frame a fighter spawns — and only lift once the incoming scene calls `Loading.done()`. That call is at the **end of `start_match()`**, not in `_ready`: `start_match` awaits a frame partway through, so `_ready` returns before the arena exists. `MIN_SHOW` keeps the screen up long enough to read as a transition rather than a flash, and `SAFETY_SECONDS` lifts it anyway if a path forgets to call `done()`. The `NS3_*` hooks that skip the menu deliberately keep the old direct scene change, so the sim and screenshot harnesses are untouched.
- **The engine's boot splash is a PNG of this screen's first frame**, not a second design. `LoadingScreen.compose()` builds the screen as a detached Control tree — `_build` calls it, and so does `tools/make_boot_splash.gd`, which shoots it to `assets/menu/background/boot_splash.png` for `application/boot_splash/*` in project.godot. **Re-run the tool after changing the loading screen** (`Godot --path godot --script res://tools/make_boot_splash.gd`, *not* `--headless`), or boot and every transition after it stop matching. Coupled facts: `boot_splash/bg_color` is `LoadingScreen.BASE_INK` by hand, because 4.7's `stretch_mode` defaults to **Keep**, which fits the image and paints that colour into the columns either side on a phone (Cover would crop 11% off the bottom of a 19.5:9 screen, which is where the title and bar are anchored); the render composes at the authored 1280x720 and shoots at 1920 through `size_2d_override`, so the type rasterizes at the larger size instead of being an upscaled bitmap; and the PNG is imported `keep` **and** named in `export_presets.cfg`'s `exclude_filter`, because the exporter adds the boot splash by path on top of the resource sweep and it otherwise ships twice (40.0 MB → 37.2 MB). The iOS launch storyboard falls back to the same two settings, so it comes along for free on the next export.
- **`ResourceLoader.load_threaded_get` CONSUMES the request.** Poll a path again after collecting it and `load_threaded_get_status` reports `THREAD_LOAD_INVALID_RESOURCE` — so a naive "re-check everything each frame" loop sees every *finished* load as a *failed* one. Collect each path once and drop it from the pending list.
- **Per-match stats are on the fighter** (`Fighter.stats`: damage, kills, cubes, goals, saves, survived). Always on, and distinct from `main.gd`'s `sim_stats`, which is a per-KIT aggregate gated behind `sim_active` and answers a different question. Nothing in `stats` is cleared by `respawn()` — a Nobles Cup death is a setback inside one match, so a carrier who scores and then dies keeps the goal. `survived` measures from `match_start` (set when the phase turns PLAYING), not from `now`, which runs for the life of the scene and would otherwise report the second match after PLAY AGAIN as the sum of both.
- **All three ways a match can end funnel through `main.gd:_show_results`** — Showdown placement, a Cup scoreline, and a net client whose own fighter went down while the host's match ran on — so they cannot drift apart. It builds a `MenuUI` card fresh each time into `results_body`; only the overlay root and its dim persist. Its optional `board` argument swaps the portrait-plus-stat-table body for a prebuilt one: Cup passes `_cup_scoreboard` (both teams, all six players, sorted goals-then-damage), Showdown and the net client pass nothing. Two details are load-bearing — `hud.move_child(results, -1)`, because the fighter health bars are added to the HUD *after* the overlay is built in `_ready`; and hiding `CupMode.HUD_GROUP`, because Cup's scoreboard sits on its own layer *above* the card, where `move_child` cannot reach it and the scoreline printed twice. Hidden rather than freed — `CupMode._build_hud` sweeps it on PLAY AGAIN.
- **A per-player table is possible in Cup and not in Showdown.** `game.fighters` never shrinks in Cup — a death parks a fighter rather than freeing it — so everyone is still there at the whistle with their stats intact. Showdown frees them on elimination, so by the results screen most of the roster is gone.
- **A net client's stat table is the HOST's numbers.** Every mutation is host-side (`deal_damage` returns early when `not authoritative`), so a client's own `stats` are all zeros; the host pushes the real damage / eliminations / cubes / survival with `_net_push_stats` just before the elimination that raises the card, and `_net_rows` prints those. The one-row survival-clock fallback is still there for when they have not arrived.
- **`MenuUI.stagger` must not be used on rows in a container.** It goes through `pop_in`, which tweens `position:y`, and a `VBoxContainer` OWNS its children's positions: `pop_in` reads `home` off a child the container has not laid out yet, records 0 for every row, and animates the whole table into one stack where only the last row drawn is visible. The card then looks like it has one row while the code says four. `main.gd:_fade_in_rows` is the results table's own stagger and fades alpha only.
- **The versus screen already animates** (cards slide and stagger, VS punches in with `TRANS_BACK`, the countdown digits scale, the mode title takes over at `PREMATCH_INTRO_AT`). It landed in `d4e6678`; do not "add" it again.

## Touch controls

**Touch controls** (`virtual_joystick.gd` = `TouchStick`, laid out by `main.gd:_layout_hud`, driven by `main.gd:_unhandled_input`) — three sticks, and every one of them both parked and floating.

- **They are on screen when nobody is touching them,** translucent, colour-coded: **blue** walks (left half), **red** shoots (right half), **gold** is the Super. They used to be invisible until touched, which is readable only once you already know they exist — nothing said the left half walks and the right half shoots, and the Super button was the only control you could see. Colour is the only thing telling them apart at a glance, so it has to be three that never read as each other.
- **They still float to the finger.** A touch anywhere in the stick's own region re-anchors the base under the thumb and the base slides home on release (`RETURN_TIME`). That is also what keeps a TAP unambiguous: `value` is zero at the instant of the press however far from home it landed, so a press-and-release is always a tap and never a full-deflection drag. The old Super button anchored its stick at the button and then fed it the touch position, so a thumb landing on the button's edge released a manually aimed Super it never asked for.
- **The Super's grab radius is much wider than the ring it draws** (`SUPER_GRAB_RADIUS` 118 against a 70 px ring, ~2.3x the old button's area). It can afford that because **an uncharged Super now falls THROUGH to the aim stick**: the old code swallowed the press, so a thumb that landed on the button in the half-second before it filled fired nothing at all, which is indistinguishable from a control that does not work.
- **There is no Super button.** `super_button.gd` is gone; the gold stick draws the charge dial and the star itself (`TouchStick.charge` >= 0 is what turns them on), so one circle does the job two overlapping ones used to.
- Sticks are hidden whenever `phase != PLAYING` or the player is down. That check sits **before** `_update_status`'s validity guard, because a Showdown elimination frees the player and would otherwise leave three parked joysticks under the results card.

## Aiming: tap, drag and the indicator

**Tap-to-fire takes the nearest visible target**, for both an attack (`auto_aim_target`) and a Super (`main.gd:_super_target`) — same reach (`range * 1.1`), same wall and bush checks. The one difference is that a Super only ever picks a FIGHTER, never a loot box; a tapped attack falls back to a box so that tapping in a quiet corner opens crates instead of firing at nothing. Nearest is the only rule a player can predict without reading a marker, and a picker that re-ranks every tap sends consecutive shots at different people, which reads as the game arguing with you. The Super was briefly an expected-value picker — target worth (would this finish them, were you already trading with them via `Fighter.engaged_with`, are they wounded, are they close) times `_connect_odds` — and **it was removed on 2026-09-06 and should not come back**: a Super is aimed under pressure at the fighter you are looking at, and any rule that quietly prefers a different one is wrong at the moment it matters however good its reasoning is. `_tap_plan` returns `{kind, dir, target}` and has two cases left, `target` and `free`. **Every tap fires**, including a Super with nobody in reach, which shoots forward — there used to be a third case, `hold`, where it kept its charge instead, and a button that decides not to go off reads as a broken button rather than as restraint. The two `free` exceptions are deliberate and must survive any change here: Pop Off leaps the way you are already running (it is an escape) and Downhill sets off rather than firing at the wall in front of you. `tools/aim_probe.gd` prints what the picker chose and every candidate it chose between.

**A tap aims at where the target IS, never where it will be, and that is the whole design.** `_tap_plan` returns `target.global_position` flat. It briefly led the target perfectly (`main.gd:_aim_lead`, removed 7 Sep 2026) to fix a real fairness bug — bots led and the player did not — and that created a worse one: **a tap that leads is strictly better than aiming**, so drawing a lane with the stick became the slow way to do what the button did for free. Brawl Stars' auto-aim fires at the current position too, which is exactly why auto-aiming a moving target at range misses, why drag-aim is the skill, and why auto-aiming a thrower is a known bad play. It is also the rule already written down one paragraph up about the aim indicator: a preview that hands you the target "makes tapping the obvious play, when aiming is meant to be the thing you get good at" — a leading tap was that same lock-on, moved out of the preview and into the shot. **Do not put the lead back.** What it costs is exactly `SHOT_FEEL.md`'s `lead/hit` column — a tap misses a full-speed strafing target by that many body-widths at full range, linearly in range, so a tap still lands up close. Tap to brawl, aim to snipe. The fairness half is answered by bots leading only 0.00-0.40 of the correct amount (`BotBrain.LEAD_MATCH_*`), because the honest comparison is bot-aim against the player's DRAG, not against their tap: a bot has no tap, `_aim_point` IS its manual aim. `BotBrain.LEAD_SIM` (0.85) must NOT move — the sim wants bots that connect so `hits/atk` measures the weapon.

**A drawn shot can be called off, and that is not a hole in "every tap fires".** `TouchStick.peak` records the furthest a stick went during the CURRENT touch (reset by `begin`), because `value` alone cannot tell a finger that never left home from one dragged out to aim and brought back — both read zero at release. So `main.gd:_release_fire` has three cases: out and released is the lane you drew, never left home is a TAP and still auto-aims at the nearest target, out and brought BACK fires nothing. The rule survives because a tap is a gesture you did not aim, whereas drawing a lane and pulling it back is a second gesture performed deliberately — and it already tells you both ways, since the aim indicator vanishes and `_update_aim_detent` fires `aim_off` at the same `TAP_THRESHOLD` crossing. Use that constant and no other, or the tell and the behaviour drift. The check sits ahead of `_kick_instead`, so a Cup carrier who thinks better of a kick keeps the ball.

**The aim indicator only ever draws what a DRAG would do** (`main.gd:_draw_weapon_aim` — the weapon's real cone, lane, arc or weave, at the direction and reach the stick is asking for). A finger that is down but not yet dragged draws **nothing at all**, and that is the point: it used to preview the auto-aim's pick as a lane to the chosen fighter plus a ring around their feet, which is a lock-on marker — it hands you the target for free and makes tapping the obvious play, when aiming is meant to be the thing you get good at. That lane was also why **Nova's indicator turned into a thin straight line** part of the time: the line to a target is drawn identically for every kit and has nothing to do with the shape the weapon covers, so a shotgunner holding the stick saw a rifle's lane. Nobles Cup's carrier is the one exception (`_draw_kick_aim` still marks a tapped kick's destination) because that ring disambiguates a pass from a shot at goal — two different actions — rather than pointing out a target. `NS3_AIM_SHOW=attack|super` holds the drag indicator on at full deflection along the player's facing, since the indicator is otherwise only up while a finger is on the glass and `NS3_SHOTS` could never catch it.

## Effects

**Match feel** — the effects layer, and the two rules it follows.

- **Effects are ImmediateMesh, not GPUParticles3D.** `shockwave.gd`, `melee_swipe.gd` and `hit_spark.gd` all rebuild a mesh per frame from vertex colours, unshaded, and free themselves. The camera is a fixed steep top-down, so a spray drawn flat in XZ reads from the only angle anyone sees, costs one draw call, and ships no art file — the same reasoning that makes every sound synthesized.
- **Two things will make a new effect invisible while rendering perfectly.** It needs `no_depth_test` if it sits on a fighter (a burst at chest height is hidden by that fighter's own body at a 60° camera pitch), and it needs to be **big**: the camera shows ~23 m across 1280 px, about 55 px/m, and a fighter is only ~70 px wide, so sub-metre geometry is a handful of pixels. `HitSpark`'s first pass at 1.5 m with darts spanning half of that was measurably spawning and completely unseeable.
- **A brief effect cannot be tuned from a screenshot.** Five frames is nothing. Hold it open — set the duration to ~1.5s temporarily, shoot it, then put it back. The same trick is how `Fighter.FLASH_ENERGY` was set.
- **Per-fighter visual state must not touch the GLB's own materials.** They live on a Mesh *resource* shared by every fighter wearing that model, so `_setup_model` duplicates each surface material and installs it as a surface override; the hit flash and bush fade drive those copies. The flash is **emission**, since `albedo_color` multiplies the texture and white is a no-op on a textured character. **Concealment is a TINT, not a fade, and that is load-bearing.** A character is a closed solid, so every per-fragment transparency mode shows you its own far side — the inside of a skull through a face, Sanjit's staff through his chest, shoes through shins. `TRANSPARENCY_ALPHA_DEPTH_PRE_PASS` is the textbook answer and is what this used, and **the prepass does nothing under the Forward Mobile renderer this project runs on**: every model came out a smear the moment it stood in a bush, and it shipped that way because it was never shot standing in one. `TRANSPARENCY_ALPHA_HASH` does fix the sort (opaque pass, stochastic discard, real depth) but dithers, and at any alpha low enough to read as hidden the sparkle on a moving fighter is worse than the bug. So a concealed fighter stays opaque and multiplies its albedo by `CONCEAL_TINT` instead — no sorting, no dithering, and the tell still reads because the foliage around you opens up at the same time. `set_concealed` runs every physics frame for every fighter, so `Fighter._conceal_applied` still gates it to real transitions.
- **`main.gd:clear_in_flight()`** frees everything a weapon has put in the air (and `DisconnectZone`/`Shockwave`/`MeleeSwipe`), shared by `start_match` and `CupMode.kickoff` so the lists cannot drift. It deliberately excludes the Ball, which a kickoff re-places. Freeing a `Boomerang` is also what returns Sanjit's staff (`boomerang.gd:_exit_tree`).
- **Going down and coming back are code, not clips.** No GLB here ships a death or a hit animation — the models carry idle, walk, run and an attack — so `Fighter._pop_out` swells the body for a beat and bursts it, and `_play_arrival` grows a bubble around a fighter scaling up out of nothing and then pops it. Both act on `_model` (or the capsule), never on the fighter: `rotation.y` on the fighter is its FACING and aim, bars and the ball's carry point all read it. A first pass toppled the body onto the floor instead; a pop is better because at a 60° camera a body lying down is a shape you have to read, and the point of the moment is that it is instant. The bubble is additive and never writes depth, so the fighter growing inside is never hidden by it, and its fresnel is deliberately far thicker than a real one — at 55 px/m a shell a few degrees wide is a couple of pixels, and the first version was invisible against the pale end zone a Cup respawn happens in. A Cup knock-out hides the body at the END of the pop rather than on the frame of the hit; `fighter_bars.gd:77` already skips a dead fighter, so no full health bar hangs over it.
- **A Nobles Cup death washes the screen red and counts you back in** (`main.gd:_build_down_overlay`, `_update_down_overlay`, `DOWN_SHADER`). Two things it learned: a FLAT red pane at an alpha low enough to keep the pitch readable does not read as red at all — over green it composites to olive and the screen just looks dirty — so it is a vignette, saturated at the edges and nearly clear in the middle; and the counter sits in the lower third rather than dead centre, because `center_label` is there and a kickoff's "GO!" printed straight through it. It is Cup-only: a Showdown death has nothing to count to and raises the results card instead. It also cannot reach over Cup's own scoreboard, which is on its own CanvasLayer above the HUD — the same thing `_show_results` works around, and desirable here, since the score and clock stay legible through the wash.
- **`main.gd:focus_camera(at, seconds)`** points the camera somewhere other than the player for a beat, on a slower lerp than the player follow. Cup uses it on a goal, inside the kickoff freeze that already holds input.

## Match lineup

**Match lineup** (`main.gd:lineup_kits` / `:next_bot_name`, called from `start_match` and `CupMode.build_match`) — who you actually fight.

- **Kits are dealt from a shuffled pool without replacement, never `pick_random()` per slot.** Per-slot random put two of the same character on one Nobles Cup team about a third of the time and dealt Showdown the same fighter three or four times. `lineup_kits(count, first)` refills the pool when it runs dry rather than capping, so Showdown's ten slots against nine kits fill nine of them with nine different characters and only the tenth can echo. **Cup deals one lineup PER SIDE**, so no character repeats within a team but the same character may line up opposite themselves — Brawl Ball's own rule, and deliberate. `first` (the player's kit) is dealt out of the first pass only, and comparison is by NAME: `player_kit()` returns Session's own copy of the dictionary, not one of the instances `Kits.all()` builds fresh on every call, so `==` would never match.
- **Bots wear usernames** from `game.json`'s `opponents` (56 of them, via `MenuData.opponent_names`), dealt without repeats from a per-match shuffle that `start_match` clears. `display_name` prints in four places at once — the versus cards, the elimination feed, the nameplate over the fighter and the results table. **`NS3_SIM` keeps the old kit-based naming on purpose**, because `[sim] match 12/60: Kovacs 4 wins` is the line that has to stay readable.
- **The Cup kickoff spots are shuffled once into `CupMode._lineup`, and `build_match` and `kickoff` both read that array** — never `arena.team_spawns` directly. The player is always team 0 slot 0, so without the shuffle they opened every single match on the same tile at the left end of the row. The two callers agreeing is load-bearing: when they disagreed, fighters spawned on top of each other and the capsule depenetration fired them off the pitch and several hundred metres into the air.
- **`fighter_bars.gd`'s stack hangs UP from `HEAD_OFFSET`, not down.** It used to hang down from 3.4 m, so the clearance had to be re-earned every time a row was added, and by the time the health bar, the ammo pips and Hammy's heat pips were stacked it was being painted across the model's face. The anchor is now the top of the head (2.75 m) plus a fixed `HEAD_CLEAR` in pixels, and every row grows upward from there, which makes a new row free. Anything added below the pips has to be counted into `below`.

## The hitbox ring

**Every fighter wears a hitbox ring** (`fighter.gd:_setup_hitbox_ring`), in every mode, not just Nobles Cup. The torus straddles `FIGHTER_RADIUS`, so its mid-line is 1.40 m against a 1.30 m tile. It takes the team colour in Cup and the kit colour in Showdown — the same split `fighter_bars.gd` makes for health bars. It is the only thing that makes the tile-is-a-fighter unit legible while playing, because the models are roughly half the capsule's width.
