# Character models

The Meshy GLB pipeline and worn gear. Split out of `CLAUDE.md`.

## The Meshy pipeline

**Character model pipeline:** Meshy exports GLB — always run `python3 Tools/fix_meshy_glb.py <file.glb>` on a fresh export before committing (fixes broken material export, +Z facing, and adds the missing Idle clip); cleaned models live in `Assets/3D/` (see its README) and get copied into `godot/assets/` when wired into a kit. The synthesized Idle is a stance, not the rest pose: `--idle-from CLIP:SECONDS` takes a frame of an existing clip as the base, `--idle-aim BONE:x,y,z` points a bone (its +Y) along a world direction (parents before children; the model faces **-Z**, so "forward" is negative z) and `--idle-spin BONE:ax,ay,az:deg` turns one about its own axis; `--replace-idle` drops the old Idle so all of that can be re-run on an already-fixed file. Ayaan's is `Punch_Combo_3` at 0.0 (symmetric feet, fists) with both arms re-aimed down-forward to hip height and an 8° forward lean — the guard frame it started from read as hands-up surrender on the home screen. Look at a stance with `tools/pose_probe.gd` (`NS3_POSE_KIT=ayaan NS3_POSES="Idle:0,Idle:0:front"`; `front` is the menu stage's straight-on view) before wiring it. A model whose fighter throws what it holds (Sanjit's staff) is re-exported with `--split-held-item left|right`, which carves the item into a `held_item` node; `fighter.gd:set_held_item_visible` hides it while the thrown copy flies and `boomerang.gd:_exit_tree` puts it back. Meshy's run/attack clips drop the hips below the rest pose, so `fighter.gd` calibrates each model's idle foot height at spawn and lifts the model per-frame by however far the lowest foot bone sinks below it; `Godot --path godot --headless --script res://tools/foot_probe.gd` prints the per-clip sink and lift for every model.

## Worn gear

**Worn gear** (`kits.gd` `gear`, `fighter.gd:_setup_gear`): a prop bolted to bones for part of a clip — Ayaan's skis through the Downhill jump (`"on": "super"`), Anders' hacky sack on his kicking foot from the cast until the kick lands (`"on": "attack"`, then `main.gd:_launch_sack` throws the same model from `gear_global_position()`). A piece is either a named node of the gear GLB (`{"node": "left", "bone": "LeftFoot"}` — `fix_meshy_glb.py --split-halves` carves a pair into `left`/`right`) or, with no `node`, the whole model fitted to `radius` by `Fighter.fit_ball` (which is also what `hacky_sack.gd` uses for the thrown copy when `weapon.model` names a GLB). Each piece hangs off a `BoneAttachment3D` and its rest transform is **solved in world space** — `skeleton.global_transform * get_bone_global_rest(bone)` inverted against the pose the kit asks for in model space. Solving in skeleton space looked right on the ground and put the skis a hundred metres away at the apex of the jump, because Meshy armatures carry a 0.01 scale that a child of the skeleton inherits. `rotation_deg`/`scale`/`offset` are in the model's frame with `offset.y` an absolute height. `Loading.to_match` preloads gear and `weapon.model` GLBs beside the bodies for the same reason it preloads them. **Judge it with `tools/gear_probe.gd`** (`NS3_GEAR_SHOTS="ayaan:Jump_Over_Obstacle:0.8,anders:Kick_a_Soccer_Ball:0.3"`), which runs the identical solve on a frozen frame — the live wind-up is 0.12 s long.
